# kloudcourse.in: Courses Students Need, 1st Year to Final Year

Researched: 23 Nov 2026. Audience: Indian BTech students, mainly CSE/IT, with a few courses that work for every branch.

---

## 1. What the evidence says

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate employability in India | 54.81% in 2025, 56.35% in 2026 | India Skills Report (CII / Wheebox / AICTE / AIU) |
| Candidates who can write correct logic for a program | 4.77% | Aspiring Minds study, reported by India Today |
| Engineers unemployable in the knowledge economy | about 80%, with no improvement in 9 years | Aspiring Minds National Employability Report 2019 |
| Engineers employable for software jobs at start-ups | 3.84% | Aspiring Minds NER 2019 |
| Engineers with new-age skills (AI/ML, data, mobile) | about 3% | Aspiring Minds NER 2019 |
| IT graduates with adequate coding skills | about 10% | Statista chart |
| Most employable group | Management graduates at 78%, ahead of engineering | ISR 2025 |
| Gig workforce projection | 23.5 million by 2030 | ISR 2025/26 coverage |
| Women vs men employability | 54% vs 51.5% | ISR 2026 |
| What placement tests check | English communication, logical and numerical reasoning, problem-solving, coding | AMCAT research paper (ScienceDirect) |

**Plain reading:** students do not lack degrees or certificates. They lack:
1. the ability to write working code and solve problems,
2. communication and aptitude for placement tests, and
3. hands-on experience building and shipping real projects.

### Gaps in existing platforms
These come from known platform coverage, not a formal audit.
- **NPTEL / SWAYAM:** good theory, mapped to the syllabus, but light on hands-on building. Students watch rather than do.
- **Coursera / Udemy:** generic and global. They are not mapped to the Indian semester calendar, the placement season, viva or internship rules.
- **YouTube:** free and huge, but scattered. There is no path, no feedback and no proof of skill.
- **MIT "Missing Semester":** strong on tools (terminal, Git), but not India-focused and not packaged for first-years.
- **Nobody covers the final-year project end to end.** That means the idea, dataset, baselines, proposed model, app, report, PPT and viva. Students pay project centres for this today, which is your clearest opening.

## 2. Year-wise course catalog

Each course lists its **purpose** (what the student can do after it) and **why it is needed** (the evidence or pain point).

### Year 1: Foundations
| # | Course | Purpose | Why students need it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Missing Semester: Laptop, Linux, Git and VS Code** | Use a terminal, install tools, push code to GitHub, debug setup errors. | Colleges assume these skills but never teach them. Every later course, internship and project depends on them, and first-years lose weeks to "it doesn't run on my laptop". |
| 2 | **Programming by Solving Problems (C/Python)** | Turn a problem statement into working logic, not just memorised syntax. | Only 4.77% can write correct program logic and only about 10% of IT grads code adequately. This is the root gap. |
| 3 | **Tech English and Communication** | Write emails, explain code aloud, present, and handle group discussions. | AMCAT and placement rounds test English communication. It is a major filter for Tier-2/3 college students. |
| 4 | **How to Learn Engineering: Study Systems, Notes, Exams** | Plan semesters, use spaced practice, prepare for exams, avoid backlogs. | The jump from school to college causes backlogs. A short, cheap course works as a good entry product for first-years. |

### Year 2: Core Skills
| # | Course | Purpose | Why students need it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **DSA Made Practical** | Arrays to graphs with pattern-based problem solving and 150 curated problems. | The coding round is the first filter in almost every IT placement. Only 3.84% are start-up employable. |
| 6 | **DBMS and SQL Hands-on** | Design schemas, write real queries, and connect a database to an app. | SQL is asked in nearly every interview (TCS, Infosys, analytics roles), and it is needed for the final-year project app. |
| 7 | **OOP and Clean Code (Java/Python)** | Classes, design and readable code, with refactoring practice. | Viva and interviews ask OOP concepts. Clean code is what internships expect. |
| 8 | **Web Development Foundations** | HTML, CSS, JS and one framework, ending with a deployed portfolio site. | It is the fastest visible proof of skill and is needed for internships and project UIs. |
| 9 | **Aptitude and Reasoning (start early)** | Quantitative, logical and verbal ability at placement-test level. | AMCAT/eLitmus/TCS NQT filter on aptitude before coding. Starting in 2nd year avoids last-minute panic. |

### Year 3: Build, Ship, Get Hired
| # | Course | Purpose | Why students need it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Build and Deploy a Full-Stack App** | Auth, database, API and cloud deployment, ending with a live app on a resume. | It closes the "can't build real things" gap. Recruiters ask "show me something you built". |
| 11 | **Cloud and DevOps Basics** | Linux servers, Docker, CI/CD, and AWS/Azure free tier. | This fits kloudcourse's brand, but as a practical skill rather than "cloud theory". Very few graduates have it. |
| 12 | **AI/ML Foundations and Responsible AI-Tool Use** | Train, evaluate and compare models honestly. Use ChatGPT/Copilot without cheating yourself. | About 3% have new-age skills. India's large AI-talent pool is growing, and every student now uses AI tools but few use them well. |
| 13 | **Internship Kit: Resume, LinkedIn, GitHub, Applying** | Build an ATS-ready resume, portfolio and outreach messages, and get an internship. | Internships are part of the AICTE curriculum (*unverified: exact current hours/credits*). Students don't know how or where to apply. |
| 14 | **Open-Source Contribution** | Make a first real PR, then GSoC/Hacktoberfest-style contributions. | It is strong resume proof and teaches teamwork through Git. |

### Year 4: Final Year
| # | Course | Purpose | Why students need it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | **⭐ Final-Year Project: Idea to Viva** | Choose the topic, find the dataset, build 3 baselines and 1 proposed model, report real results, build the full app (auth and DB), then the report, PPT and viva Q&A. | No platform covers this end to end, and students pay project centres. It matches your existing strength exactly. **This is the flagship course.** |
| 16 | **Interview Bootcamp** | Technical, HR and managerial rounds, with mock interviews and company-wise patterns. | Many students pass the test and then fail the interview. Communication is a known weak point. |
| 17 | **System Design Basics** | Scaling, caching, databases and APIs at fresher level. | Product companies now ask lite system design even of freshers. |
| 18 | **Writing a Research Paper (IEEE/Scopus)** | Literature survey, paper structure, results tables, choosing a journal, avoiding predatory journals. | Many colleges require a paper from final-year projects, and students get scammed by fake journals. |

### All Years: Life Skills
| # | Course | Purpose | Why students need it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | **Freelancing and the Gig Economy** | Find clients, price work, deliver and get paid safely. | India's gig workforce is projected at 23.5M by 2030. It offers income during college. |
| 20 | **Personal Finance for Students** | First salary, tax basics, budgeting, avoiding loan apps and scams. | Nobody teaches it, and first-salary mistakes are common. Keep it educational, with no stock tips. |

### Later: Non-CSE tracks
ECE (embedded, IoT, VLSI basics), Mechanical (CAD, EV basics) and a GATE/higher-studies roadmap. Add these after the CSE catalog is proven.

## 3. What to launch first

| Priority | Course | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Final-Year Project: Idea to Viva | Biggest unmet need, highest willingness to pay, and your unique strength |
| 2 | The Missing Semester | Cheap entry course that reaches 1st-years early, who then stay for 4 years |
| 3 | Build and Deploy a Full-Stack App | Feeds into the project course and matches the "cloud" brand |
| 4 | DSA Made Practical and Aptitude | Steady demand every placement season |

**Full outline of course #15:** [final-year-project-course-outline.md](final-year-project-course-outline.md)

**Format for every course:** short videos, a hands-on lab per module, quizzes, one capstone, and a certificate tied to a real, viewable artifact (GitHub repo or live link) rather than a completion-only certificate.
